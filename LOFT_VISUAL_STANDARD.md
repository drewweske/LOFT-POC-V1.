## 1. Governing idea

LOFT is stylized realism: a believable golf world edited into large, memorable,
ownable forms. It is not photoreal simulation, toy geometry, or a generic mobile
golf skin. Every surface should feel deliberately authored by one creative direction.

The recognizable LOFT grammar is:

- **Sculpted simplicity** — large readable forms, controlled detail, low noise.
- **Curved language** — soft but confident arcs across land, architecture, people,
  clubs, bags, balls, and interface geometry.
- **Honest materials** — matte or satin responses, subtle wear, believable weight,
  no plastic gloss masquerading as quality.
- **Warm natural light** — directional, time-of-day-aware light with readable
  silhouettes and atmospheric depth.
- **Signal with restraint** — orange identifies action, selection, impact, or one
  signature detail. It is never wallpaper.
- **Human first** — golfers, rivals, caddies, groundskeepers, marshals, and locals;
  never fantasy classes in golf clothing.
- **A distinct place** — every course is a real-feeling biome with its own terrain,
  ecology, weather, and built history inside one LOFT universe.

## 2. The zero-brand requirement

Remove the logo, brand typography, and orange accent. Then test:

1. **Face** — does the face still feel LOFT?
2. **Silhouette** — can the role or object be recognized at gameplay distance?
3. **Gameplay frame** — would a raw screen without UI plausibly be recognized as
   LOFT?
4. **Course frame** — does the environment alone carry a distinct identity?
5. **Object** — do club, ball, bag, flag, tee marker, bench, sign, and prop belong to
   one industrial-design family?
6. **Mixed world** — do character, equipment, architecture, terrain, and vegetation
   look art-directed together?

Failure without the brand means the underlying design is generic.

## 3. Shape and composition

Favor broad masses, confident negative space, controlled asymmetry, and a hierarchy
readable at phone size. A scene should have foreground texture, playable midground,
a destination landmark, and atmospheric background. Avoid evenly scattering detail.

Curves must be purposeful. Terrain shoulders, bunker lips, shrub masses, rooflines,
club soles, faces, and UI corners can rhyme without becoming identical pills. Hard
edges are reserved for structure, machining, score rules, and decisive interaction.

The world owns the frame. Gameplay UI should usually occupy roughly the quiet edge of
the composition rather than masking the route, golfer, target, hazards, or elevation.

## 4. Color and material language

Core interface colors:

- **Clubhouse Ink** `#0B0D0D` — structure, field instruments, equipment stage.
- **Scorecard Cream** `#F2EFE8` — editorial space and legible contrast.
- **Fairway Stone** `#B8B1A6` — neutral hierarchy and tactile secondary surfaces.
- **Flag Orange** `#FF6A2A` — action, selection, impact, and restrained signature.

World color is richer and place-specific but never fluorescent. Grass cuts should be
separated by value, texture scale, blade height, and directional response—not a flat
palette swap. Metals read as brushed steel, forged chrome, black titanium, coated
carbon, or ceramic. Fabric reads matte and woven. Skin reads warm and diffuse. Stone,
soil, wood, sand, and painted architecture retain their physical character.

Avoid generic gradients, emissive rarity, broad orange panels, chrome-everything,
plastic skin, mirror turf, and identical roughness across unrelated materials.

## 5. Lighting and atmosphere

Use warm, low-to-mid directional light, soft sky contribution, readable contact
shadows, and coastal atmospheric depth. Time of day must contribute mood and grade
legibility. Silhouettes remain readable against sky and ocean; near terrain keeps
enough contrast to judge slope.

Cloud, ocean, foliage, flag, and distant haze may move quietly. Nothing should wobble
for attention. Avoid flat ambient wash, crushed black fairways, hard white fog,
overexposed cream UI, and lighting that erases ball or cup contact.

## 6. Coastal Ridge world language

Coastal Ridge is a premium golf destination shaped by wind, drainage, rock, and
maintenance—not an extruded lane. Its route should reveal safe and ambitious lines,
protected landing zones, a legible green complex, and memorable coastal landmarks.

- **Macro landform:** long shoulders, shelves, saddles, swales, crowns, and collection
  areas. Micro-noise never substitutes for authored earth movement.
- **Fairway:** intentional widths and contour; directional mowing reinforces route
  and grade.
- **First cut / rough:** physical blade-height and density progression with organic,
  maintained edges.
- **Green / fringe:** tight material, readable crown and break, guarded but believable
  transitions.
- **Bunker:** excavated bowl, raised turf lip, sand depth and grain; never a beige decal.
- **Coast:** layered cliff geology, tide/foam relationship, wind exposure, salt-tolerant
  planting, and clear water threshold.
- **Vegetation:** clustered ecological communities, not evenly randomized primitives.
- **Architecture:** warm, restrained clubhouse and lighthouse forms that share local
  materials and scale.

If a slope moves the ball, it must be readable through silhouette, light, mowing,
surface texture, or an intentionally subtle gameplay aid. Rendered and physical
terrain must agree exactly.

## 7. Character language

LOFT people are slightly stylized, anatomically coherent, calm, and role-specific.

Face DNA:

- strong brow plane and thick natural eyebrows
- almond-shaped, restrained horizontal eyes
- medium bridge and simplified angular nose
- soft square jaw, understated lips, warm matte skin
- sculpted hair masses and subtle asymmetry
- neutral, focused, confident, relaxed, determined, or lightly reactive expression

Avoid giant round eyes, anime proportions, tiny noses, theatrical smiles, perfect
symmetry, glossy/waxy skin, and generic mannequin faces.

Bodies need a continuous skeleton, believable joints, grounded feet, functional
clothing layers, and a silhouette that identifies role. Club Pro is composed and
athletic; Rival is long and competitive; Caddie is practical and mobile;
Groundskeeper is weighty and work-worn; Marshal is stable and authoritative; Rookie
is younger and slightly looser.

Swing motion must transmit force through feet → legs → hips → torso → shoulders →
arms → hands → club. Address, takeaway, top, transition, delivery, impact, release,
finish, and putting stroke remain connected. Hands retain a plausible grip; joints do
not float, telescope, snap, or independently orbit.

## 8. Club and equipment language

Equipment is a collectible hero system. The physical object appears large enough to
admire; the UI acts as its gallery and comparison tool.

Each family owns intentional head geometry, face, sole, cavity/crown, weighting,
hosel, shaft, grip, machining, edge treatment, finish, and wear:

- **Driver:** powerful swept volume, controlled crown, engineered sole and face.
- **Woods / hybrids:** shallower progression with distinct launch and turf language.
- **Irons:** forged cavity or muscle architecture with precise leading edge and hosel.
- **Wedges:** compact head, high-friction face, sole/bounce made visibly purposeful.
- **Putter:** alignment, face, neck, weighting, and ground relationship are primary.

Progression is industrial design:

- **Low level:** ordinary construction, broad shapes, fewer parts, visible utility and
  wear.
- **Mid level:** cleaner fit, improved shaping, better materials and restraint.
- **Level 50:** engineered, premium, desirable, with clear performance intent.
- **Level 75:** rare, highly refined, sophisticated construction and exceptional
  silhouette.
- **Signature/endgame:** iconic without a label, glow, rarity frame, or logo.

Recolor-only rarity is prohibited.

## 9. LOFT ball

The playable physics ball is the hero ball; no separate generic gameplay sphere is
allowed.

Target approximately 338 controlled dimples with seamless staggered flow and
four-axis visual balance. Dimples have deliberate bowl geometry, matte microtexture,
soft specular rolloff, and enough shadow to read at inspection distance without
becoming noisy during flight.

The cover is premium off-white. Exactly one fixed orange signature dimple belongs at
the authored lower-left-quadrant reference (normalized direction approximately
`-0.382, -0.382, 0.813`). It is never randomized, emissive, glowing, or multiplied.
LOD may simplify distant geometry, but the actual gameplay object retains the same
identity and orientation contract.

## 10. Cup, flag, and small course objects

The cup is a spatial golf hole: a regulation-scale mouth, turf cut, subtle rim, inner
wall, darkness/depth, bottom, and a coherent flagstick relation. The ball must cross
the lip, descend, become occluded by the ground, and settle below the surface. A flat
black decal, white target disc, magnetic capture, or ball resting visibly on the rim
fails.

Flags, tee markers, benches, signs, bins, and range objects use the same curved,
honest, restrained physical language. Orange flags may signal the target; unrelated
props should not repeat orange decoratively.

## 11. UI and interaction

LOFT UI is a quiet, golf-native field layer. It informs, never competes.

- Use scorecard hierarchy, yardage, flags, club silhouettes, round context, and
  course-marking logic without themed gimmicks.
- Use ink for structure, cream for editorial reading, stone for hierarchy, and orange
  for the one active state.
- Prefer a few strong instruments and receipts over grids of generic cards.
- Typography is clean, confident, compact, and precise; numbers carry gameplay
  hierarchy.
- Motion is quick, calm, and meaningful: settle, reveal, confirm, leave.
- Touch targets and text remain usable on mobile without allowing panels to cover the
  shot composition.

Avoid glassmorphism, random gradients, neon, giant glow, browser-default controls,
React-dashboard composition, repeated pill containers, dense HUD, excessive cards,
and “AI app” ornament.

## 12. Camera and gameplay presentation

The camera presents golfer, ball, target, hazards, elevation, and surrounding route
as one premium composition. Movement is damped, predictable, and responsive. Aim,
swing, flight, landing, result, and transition are continuous rather than separate
camera tricks.

Putting permits deliberate close inspection of ball, cup, break, surface grain, and
target line. Precision cannot be blocked by an arbitrary zoom floor or forced camera
jump. Spatial target aids are restrained, terrain-attached, and informative—not giant
arcade rings.

## 13. Animation and life

Human motion emphasizes weight transfer, anticipation, connected follow-through, and
quiet recovery. World motion is low-amplitude and place-driven: coastal wind, flag
response, ocean rhythm, foliage drift, and cloud travel. UI animation communicates
state rather than decorating it.

## 14. Anti-pattern gate

Reject work that is merely different, busier, or technically clever. Explicitly
reject:

- disconnected geometric limbs or floating joints
- flat cups, floating balls, clipping clubs, and mismatched contact
- low-poly faceting exposed as the primary terrain language
- scattered vegetation with no ecology or composition
- color-only equipment tiers
- generic sphere plus orange dot as the final ball
- generic SaaS cards, excessive pills, glass, neon, and glow
- multiple independent art styles on one gameplay screen
- overlays that hide the world or controls that obstruct precision
- surface noise that implies physics the collider does not contain

The final question is always: without logo, orange, or explanation, does this object,
person, interface, and landscape still deserve to be called LOFT?
